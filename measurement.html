How we measure

We are highly ambitious for every child, and realistic about what the next step looks like in each school. Context tells us where to aim next; it never lowers the aspiration.

Every school, every person matters

Every school is different, and that matters

Every school and setting in Nova is different, and every person in them matters. Each serves a different community, with its own contextual strengths and its own barriers. We understand those differences, and we build our measures around them. A single national benchmark applied flatly would be unfair to some schools and unambitious for others. So instead of one set of trust-wide targets, we set targets that fit each school, and hold ourselves to them.

School target sheets

Setting-specific targets, captured in school target sheets

Each school has a target sheet. It uses what we already know about the school alongside DfE data on similar schools (the 50-comparator set) and the school’s IDSR. From these we set realistic targets for the short, medium and long term, so progress is manageable and every step forward is recognised.

01

Short term

Match or beat similar schools. The first, reachable step.

02

Medium term

Reach national. Building momentum year on year.

03

Long term

Exceed national. Ambitious, and earned by getting there in stages.

Highly ambitious, realistic about context. Ignoring a school’s context would set it up to fail, and that is demotivating. Recognising it is how we make ambition achievable.

What we measure

We measure results

The target sheet holds results, the outcomes we commit to for children: attainment and progress across the curriculum, the gaps we are closing, attendance (an outcome for the child, not just a process), and destinations. Teamwork and Innovation are how we achieve these results. They are evidenced through quality assurance, the SEF and external review, not reduced to a score, because counting participation tells us little about impact.

Why we measure carefully

Why we measure carefully

There is a well-known risk with targets, named after the economist Charles Goodhart: when a measure becomes a target, it can stop being a good measure. People optimise for the number rather than the thing the number stands for, narrowing attention to borderline pupils, teaching to the test, or improving one figure while the real work slips. We take this seriously. We guard against it in four ways.

1

A basket, not a headline

We look across a range of outcomes, so no single number can be gamed into telling the wrong story.

2

Numbers with judgement

Every measure sits alongside professional judgement and the SEF, never on its own.

3

Moderated targets

Targets are set by the Executive Leadership group and moderated across the trust, so they are fair and consistent.

4

Watching for distortion

We actively look for the behaviours a target can provoke, and we value the outcome, not the optics.

How it joins up

From self-evaluation to action, reported to trustees

The target sheet does not sit on its own. It is informed by the school’s SEF, and it informs the School Improvement Plan and the school’s action planning. Targets are set and moderated by the Executive Leadership group. Performance against them is reported to trustees. The result is a single, honest thread: the school knows itself, sets ambitious and realistic targets, acts on them, and is held to account for the outcomes that matter for children.

Ambition and realism

Not opposites

Ambition and realism are not opposites. Knowing a school’s context is how we set targets that stretch it and that it can actually reach.
